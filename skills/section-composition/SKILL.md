# Compose one idea per section

## Mental model

A compelling section joins its message and visual rather than placing them in unrelated boxes. Clear content priority, proportion, and supporting details give the eye a coherent path.

## When this skill applies

Use when a feature section feels generic, crowded, or disconnected from the claim it is supposed to support.

## Reasoning

Choose one idea to demonstrate. Decide what the visual must prove and what the text must explain. Crop or compose the visual around the relevant detail, preserving the main reading area.

Treat background effects, labels, and decorative marks as supporting material. Build a static, readable composition first. If motion is added, use it to direct attention without requiring someone to wait for the meaning.

## Worked example — original implementation adaptation

A collaboration feature section

Before: a broad paragraph sits beside a full application screenshot whose useful detail is too small.

After: a cropped example focuses on a collaborator’s resolved comment. A short headline states the outcome; a restrained contextual caption explains the state.

## Failure modes

- A screenshot so small it communicates no product evidence.

- Decorative shapes crossing the text’s reading area.

- A composition that only works at one fixed viewport.

## Evidence that would support the decision

Inspect the section independently, at multiple widths, and with motion disabled. Confirm that the visual actually supports the stated idea.
