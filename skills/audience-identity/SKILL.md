# Make the brand belong to its audience

## Mental model

Brand cues can connect a product to the world its audience recognizes. Imagery, type, color, texture, and motion form an identity when their choices reinforce the same context.

## When this skill applies

Use when a site looks competent but interchangeable, or its visuals contradict the audience named in its copy.

## Reasoning

Collect concrete audience context: working environment, vocabulary, tools, materials, and expectations. Derive a small identity direction from those cues and explain why it fits. Keep layouts and identity choices separate enough to iterate.

Choose a few recurring visual motifs. Make images support the product and leave room for readable copy. Keep the design coherent at section boundaries, while using contrast and restraint to maintain hierarchy.

## Worked example — original implementation adaptation

Restaurant software branding

Before: a sterile software template uses generic laptop imagery and indistinguishable feature icons.

After: an identity study uses service context, menu-like typography for display, and restrained material cues. The product screenshots remain legible and the final copy stays live text.

## Failure modes

- Choosing stereotyped stock imagery without product relevance.

- Adding texture that makes text harder to read.

- Confusing a distinctive identity with a license to break the user flow.

## Evidence that would support the decision

Compare multiple sections and mobile crops. Ask whether the audience and product promise remain clear without decorative explanations.
