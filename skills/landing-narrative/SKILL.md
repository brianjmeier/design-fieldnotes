# Build a landing page as an argument

Based on Kole Jain’s UX/UI tutorials. These are unofficial, AI-assisted study notes and adapted skills; they were not written, approved or endorsed by Kole Jain. Permission has not been obtained from him.

[Source credit and reuse limits](../../SOURCE-MATERIAL.md)

## Mental model

A landing page must make a useful promise and substantiate it. Section order and concrete visuals can answer successive audience questions, while unrelated attractive blocks create a weak story.

## When this skill applies

Use for product pages, marketing sites, and redesigns where the offer is difficult to understand.

## Reasoning

State what the product helps a specific audience achieve. Select evidence that demonstrates the promise rather than repeating feature labels. Let each section answer a question that naturally follows from the previous one.

Make the primary next action and its commitment clear. Use truthful proof and actual product examples. Simplify generic prose before decorating it; do not invent customer logos, usage counts, or outcomes to fill the layout.

## Worked example — original implementation adaptation

A restaurant planning product

Before: “The all-in-one platform for innovation” sits above generic cards and an abstract gradient.

After: the promise names planning shifts with fewer gaps. A product example shows an understaffed service and the resolved schedule. Real evidence and a clearly scoped trial action follow.

## Failure modes

- A vague headline rescued by a long explanatory paragraph.

- Fabricated trust signals or unsupported claims.

- Beautiful visual sequences that obscure the actual product.

## Evidence that would support the decision

Ask a reader to describe the audience, outcome, evidence, and next action after exploring the page. Check the action’s actual destination and commitment.
